## Introduction: Why I do this

Governments, companies, families: these are all entities. But inside every one of them are humans. Humans come first. A group of humans forms an entity, and what the entity does comes from what those humans want.

Humans are perishable. Across time, what survives is the entity, not the people who made it. So to understand anything that lasts, I look at the people who built it, the people who run it now, and what drives them.

## Introduction: Why I do this

I look for this in India, among the people building its trains, its industry and its technology. Nation building and technology building both come down to people and what they aspire to.

This is one night of that, step by step. A train maker and Tata are the examples this time. Each step adds one piece to the map.

## Conclusion: What I take away

Back to where I started: humans come first, the entities they build outlast them, and what drives the builders shapes what lasts. Here's what I take away.

One: what a country can build itself is its standing in the world. The "Indian manufacturer" builds a Russian design; the first Vande Bharat was our own.

Two: geopolitics sits behind every big project, India with Russia, India with France.

## Conclusion: What I take away

Three: owning isn't controlling. The Mistrys own almost a fifth of Tata Sons and still can't sell.

Four: debt sits with people. One line, 100% pledged, said more than the price.

Five: when the one person who decides goes, power splits. After Ratan there were three centres.

Six: vision decides structure. Trusts first meant Tata Sons stayed private.
